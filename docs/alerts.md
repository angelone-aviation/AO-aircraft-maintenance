# Alerts, urgent alerts and expiring dates

Alerts tell a member what needs attention in their Flight Program: inspections, ADs and component limits coming due or passed, ADs to review, grounded aircraft, their own certificates, FAA documents to review, expiring dates, and for administrators, one member's open problem reports piling up. They appear under the bell in the header and on the **Alerts** page for everyone who can read the aircraft concerned, including in demo mode. The rules live in `src/domain/alerts.ts`; the bell and the page compute alerts from the workspace snapshot, so they work offline from the last snapshot. The page's search narrows the list by alert title, detail or aircraft registration; **Mark all read** still marks every current alert.

Each alert has a key that changes when its substance changes (the record, the due point). Read marks and sent emails are kept by key, so a changed alert is unread again and is emailed again.

A key does not change when a state ends and starts again: an aircraft marked grounded, returned to service and grounded a second time has the same alert key both times. So read marks and sent messages last for one **occurrence** of an alert, not for its key. Every server check (the urgent check and the daily digest) first forgets, for each member, the delivery rows on every channel and the read marks of alerts that member no longer has (`alerts.forgetCleared`), and the app does the same for read marks when it sees an alert gone. An alert that returns is therefore unread, emailed, pushed and listed in the digest again; while it lasts it is sent once per channel. The forgetting runs in one transaction with the alert computation, and claiming a send never forgets anything, so two checks running side by side cannot send one occurrence twice. A read mark is kept while its alert exists on any date that is today somewhere, because a device judges dates on its own calendar day.

## How alerts reach people

| Channel              | What goes out                                                                   | Who gets it                                                                                                                            | When                                                                |
| -------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Bell and Alerts page | Every alert                                                                     | Everyone with read access to the aircraft (program dates: program administrators; system dates: system administrators)                 | Always                                                              |
| Daily digest email   | Every alert not yet emailed in a digest, grouped by severity, at most 50 listed | Members whose **Email me a daily alert digest** is on (default on for Flight Program administrators)                                   | 13:00 UTC                                                           |
| Urgent alert email   | New **urgent alerts** not yet emailed on any channel, at most 10 listed         | Members whose **Email me urgent alerts at once** is on (default on for program administrators and members with maintenance permission) | Right after the change that caused them, and after the daily digest |
| Alert on this device | The same new urgent alerts as a push message, one card per program              | Devices a member switched on under **Settings → Alerts → Alert this device**                                                           | Right after the change, with the urgent email                       |

**ADs** alert by the same rule as the Overview's AD panel (`SETTLED_AD_STATUSES`): every AD the panel lists is mentioned. Each overdue or due-soon action alerts, even while the AD needs review; an AD the panel lists with no such action, one that needs review, has no due point set or whose counter cannot be read, gets one **For review** alert naming its status.

**Urgent alerts** are the kinds that need action now: an aircraft grounded, an inspection or AD overdue, a component past its limit, a date expired, a member's problem reports piling up (`URGENT_ALERT_KINDS`). Everything else waits for the digest.

**A member's problem reports piling up** (`report-backlog`) alerts a program's administrators, and no one else, once one member's open, undeferred reports reach half of either per-member limit: 100 reports or 256 KiB of text (`src/domain/reportBacklog.ts`; the limits are in [Maintenance workflows](maintenance-workflows.md#access-and-connectivity)). It names the member, opens Discrepancies, and is sent once per occurrence however many more reports arrive. A flood like bug hunt SE-13's reaches it in seconds; a genuine reporter at that point still has room, so administrators can resolve or defer reports, or check the account, before anything is refused.

**The deployment's own dates** (Settings → System → Deployment expiring dates, and the FAA key's renewal) are emailed and pushed apart from every Flight Program, once, under the name "System" ("System: 1 urgent alert", "System: 1 maintenance alert"), to every active system administrator, whether or not they hold a permission group. Each administrator's two email switches decide, as Settings shows them for the program they work in; an administrator in no group sees them off until they switch them on. A program's own emails never repeat these dates. Every other alert reaches a system administrator only through a program group, as for any member: their bell shows the alerts of the program they work in, but they are emailed and pushed a program's alerts only while they hold one of its groups, so an administrator who wants a program's alerts joins one of its groups. The system check runs after a deployment-wide date is saved, with the daily check and with the digest.

The urgent check (`convex/urgentAlerts.ts`) is scheduled by the mutations that can create an urgent state: a flight or problem report synced, a held flight accepted, an inspection, component or AD saved, an inspection template activated, a work order saved or closed, an aircraft or program saved, an aircraft, component or location transferred to another program (both programs are checked), an expiring date saved. A deployment-wide expiring date, or a new renewal date for the FAA key, schedules the system administrators' own check. It recomputes the program's alerts for each member, from what that member's groups let them see (open problem reports and deferrals included, so an aircraft grounded by a report or an expired deferral is sent like any other grounding), and sends only what is new to them. Before an email or push message is sent the alerts are **claimed** in `alertDeliveries` with their channel (`immediate` or `push`); the claim is released if the provider refuses, and the check runs again by itself 5, 15 and 60 minutes later (`RETRY_DELAYS_MS`), then waits for the next change or the daily check; a claim lasts until a check finds the alert gone. A member who gets the digest is not emailed at once for an alert the digest already listed; the digest still lists alerts that went out at once, as the day's summary.

**Whose "today".** The bell and the Alerts page judge calendar limits on the device's own date. The server has no device, so its emails, push messages and the FAA key's renewal check use the date in the deployment's **records time zone**: the Convex environment variable `RECORDS_TIME_ZONE` (an IANA name such as `America/Chicago`), US Eastern time (`America/New_York`) when it is unset. It is the same variable, with the same default, that the nightly records workbook reads from the `backups` environment ([Dropbox records copy](runbooks/dropbox-records-copy.md)); set both to the same zone. A calendar limit is overdue from the day after its due date ([GLOSSARY](../GLOSSARY.md) "Overdue"), so an "overdue" or "is grounded" message goes out once that day has begun in the records time zone, not at 00:00 UTC, which is the evening before across the Americas. A member in another time zone can see the bell change a few hours before or after the message arrives. A name that is not a time zone is logged and the default used (`convex/recordsDate.ts`).

Email goes through Resend, whose free tier sends **100 emails a day**. Immediate emails stop at **60 a day** for the whole deployment (`IMMEDIATE_EMAILS_PER_DAY` in `src/domain/alerts.ts`, which Settings quotes), counted in the `rateLimits` table under `alerts:immediate` and reset at 00:00 UTC, so the digest always fits. Only emails the provider accepted count: a refused one is given back. A capped alert waits for the digest; the check reports how many it held back. With more than 40 digest recipients, lower the cap or move Resend to a paid plan ([monitoring runbook](runbooks/monitoring.md)).

**Each Flight Program's share.** So that one program cannot spend the day's 60 and hold back another program's "is grounded" email, each program may send only its share of them (`immediateEmailShare`): the 60 split equally between the programs where an active member holds a group, never fewer than 10 (`IMMEDIATE_EMAIL_SHARE_FLOOR`). One such program gets all 60, two get 30 each, three 20, and six or more 10 each; past six the shares add up to more than 60, and the total still stops them all at 60. A program's share is counted under `alerts:immediate:<program uuid>`, beside the total, and a refused email gives back both. The deployment's own dates ("System") draw on the total alone. The first time in a UTC day that a program's share holds an email back, every active system administrator whose **Email me urgent alerts at once** switch is on gets one email, "System: <program> reached its share of urgent alert emails", and the Convex log records the program's uuid; that warning is itself an immediate email of the total (never of a program's share). The program's held-back alerts wait for the digest, as a capped alert does; the bell and alerts on devices are not held back.

Push messages use Web Push (RFC 8030, 8291, 8292) straight from a Convex Node action, with the VAPID keys in the Convex environment ([ADR-0012](adr/0012-urgent-alerts-by-web-push-and-immediate-email.md), [setup](runbooks/monitoring.md#alerts-by-email-and-on-devices)). Until `VAPID_PUBLIC_KEY`, `VAPID_PRIVATE_KEY` and `VAPID_SUBJECT` are set, the switch does not appear and nothing is sent. A device subscription belongs to the account, not to one Flight Program; whoever signs in on the device and switches alerts on takes it over. The switch reads on only while the signed-in account holds this device's subscription under the deployment's current key. Signing out releases the device, on the server and in the browser; disabling an account or resetting its password forgets every device it was alerted on. Only the browsers' own push services are accepted as a device's endpoint (`PUSH_SERVICE_HOSTS` in `src/domain/pushSubscription.ts`: Firebase Cloud Messaging for Chrome and Edge, Mozilla's for Firefox, Apple's for Safari, Windows Push Notification Services), over https on the default port without credentials; the server checks it when the device subscribes and again before each send, so no member can make the server send requests elsewhere. A browser with another push service sees "This browser's alert service is not supported. Use Chrome, Edge, Firefox or Safari." A push service that reports the subscription gone (404 or 410) has it removed; one that refuses it for good (401 or 403, as after the VAPID keys are rotated) on three checks in a row has it removed too (`MAX_PUSH_REFUSALS`), and a refusal is not retried. The device's switch then reads off, and so does a subscription made under an earlier key, so the member knows to switch it on again. The message carries the program name, the alert titles and the path to open, encrypted for the device; logs name the user id, never the endpoint or the text. On iPhone and iPad the app must be added to the Home Screen first (iOS 16.4 or later).

Privacy rules are the digest's: each member's alerts are computed from a snapshot scoped to their own groups, record text is escaped in HTML and flattened to one line in text, and a failed send logs the user id only.

## Expiring dates

An **expiring date** is a renewal the program or the deployment must not miss: insurance, registration, a service key, another secret such as an API token, or anything else. Each has a label, a kind, the date, how many days before it to warn (60 by default, up to 730), an optional note, and for a program's dates an optional aircraft.

- **Settings → Flight Program → Program expiring dates** holds the active program's dates. Flight Program administrators see, add, edit and archive them.
- **Settings → System → Deployment expiring dates** holds the deployment's own: the Cloudflare API token, `security.txt`, the VAPID keys. Only system administrators see them. The FAA DRS API key appears here as a row computed from its own renewal date, with an **Update key** action that opens the same dialog as **FAA API key and renewal**.

A date alerts from its warning window (`date-expiring`, coming due) and from the day after it passes (`date-expired`, action required, urgent), to the same people who see the list. Archiving hides a date and stops its alerts; the record and its change-log rows (`expiring-date.saved`, `expiring-date.archived`) are kept. Saving a date someone else changed is refused ("This expiring date changed. Refresh and try again."); a date is never moved between programs by editing it; a date that names an aircraft moves with that aircraft when a system administrator transfers it.

Dates are stored in `expiringItems` as JSON payloads (`src/domain/expiringItems.ts`), scoped like every other record: a member outside the program, or without administrator permission, never receives them in the snapshot. Their change-log rows follow the same rule in every history read (the snapshot's list, Change history, a date's own history and the Weekly summary): administrators see them, and a member who can read the aircraft a date names does not.

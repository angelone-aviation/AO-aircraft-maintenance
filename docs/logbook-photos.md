# Logbook page photos

For its first year the program keeps the paper aircraft logbooks beside the app. A **logbook page photo** ties an app record to the paper: a photo of the handwritten line attached to the flight, or of the signed page attached to a work order's logbook entry. The signed paper logbook stays the maintenance record ([ADR-0004](adr/0004-logbook-generator-and-status-record-system.md)); the photo only shows which paper line an app row answers to, and the monthly [paper check](reconciliation.md) lists which rows have one.

## Flights

After a flight is saved from **Log a completed flight**, **End flight** or the dashboard's **Log flight**, a notice at the top of the screen says **Flight saved** and offers **Add a photo of the logbook line**. The same control sits on every flight in the flight log (the **Logbook page** column, or the **Logbook page** block on a phone), so a photo can be added later. On a phone the control opens the rear camera; elsewhere it opens the file picker. The photo needs **flight** permission on the aircraft: the pilot who logged the flight attaches it.

A flight saved offline, or still waiting in the outbox, cannot be photographed on the server yet, because the server learns of the flight only when the outbox entry syncs. The photo is kept on the device (in its own IndexedDB database beside the JSON outbox) and shows on the flight as a thumbnail marked **Waiting**. Each photo waits on its own, even when two share a file name, as every camera capture does on an iPhone. The **Sync center** lists it as **Photo waiting** with the flight it belongs to, and uploads it on the next sync after the flight itself has reached the server. A server refusal (for example a photo of a flight the aircraft does not hold) stays on the photo as **Needs attention**, with **Sync now** to retry and **Discard photo** to drop it; a photo whose flight was discarded from the outbox is marked the same way, since nothing can file it. Like outbox entries, queued photos belong to the signed-in user and the Flight Program they were taken in, and they survive sign-out until that user signs in again.

When connected and the flight is already in the snapshot, the photo uploads at once.

## Work orders

A completed work order shows **Attach signed page photo** beside **Generate logbook entry**, for a photo of the page the certifying person signed. It needs **maintenance** permission on the aircraft. Its thumbnails appear in the same place, also in the earlier records a revised order keeps.

## Files

JPEG, PNG, WebP and PDF files up to 7 MB are accepted, the same as Form 337 copies. HEIC and HEIF, the iPhone's default photo format, are refused with the message "HEIC photos are not accepted. Choose JPEG, PNG or WebP; on an iPhone set Camera > Formats to Most Compatible." The server also reads a file's first bytes, so a file renamed to look like an accepted type (a HEIC photo named .jpg, or a web page named .pdf) is refused with "This file's contents do not match its type. Choose a PDF, JPEG, PNG or WebP file." An aircraft holds up to 500 current photos, a limit of their own: photos never use up the room kept for Form 337 copies, and archived photos do not count. A photo past the limit is refused with "This aircraft already has the maximum of 500 current logbook page photos." Each person can also upload 30 aircraft documents an hour, photos and Form 337 copies together; past that an upload is refused with "Too many aircraft document uploads. Wait an hour and try again." A queued photo refused this way shows **Needs attention** with that message, and **Sync now** sends it again.

Photos are stored on the aircraft record with the Form 337 copies (`aircraftFiles`, category `logbook-page`), each linked to its flight or work order. The server checks that the linked record is on that aircraft in the caller's Flight Program, so a photo cannot be filed against another program's flight. Everyone who reads the aircraft in the workspace sees the thumbnails; a thumbnail opens the photo full size. Aircraft owners in the owner portal and board members never receive photos: a flight line or a signed page shows flights, pilots and work those views leave out. Photos are never deleted: **Archive photo** asks for a reason and records who archived it and when, keeping the stored file. Uploads and archiving write the change-log events `aircraft.file_uploaded` and `aircraft.file_archived` with the category and link. The demo stores photos only in the browser.

The [records package](records-export.md) copies the current photos into `logbook-photos/`, and its README lists each one with the flight or work order it shows.

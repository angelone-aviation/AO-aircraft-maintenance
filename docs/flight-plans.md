# PDF flight plans

Open **Flight plans → Upload flight plan PDF**. Select the original single-page ICAO PDF from the same planning app as the supplied sample. Parsing happens in the browser using PDF.js; no external AI service or OCR is involved. The original PDF is not stored. The extracted fields, filename, and SHA-256 fingerprint are saved after review.

The review form extracts the aircraft identification and type, ICAO flight type and rules, date of flight, UTC departure, departure/destination/alternate airports, total estimated elapsed time, cruise speed/level, wrapped route, fuel endurance, persons onboard, pilot text, equipment codes, and Item 18 information. Graphical emergency-equipment cross-outs are not imported. Missing required values must be supplied before saving. This importer is specific to the fixed template; scanned and multipage PDFs are rejected.

Aircraft match only against permitted aircraft in the active Flight Program. An unmatched registration requires selection. Selecting a different aircraft requires acknowledging the mismatch. Confirm estimated cycles and landings, which default to one because this PDF does not provide them. Estimated flight duration and fuel endurance are separate fields, stored as integer minutes (0453 is 293 minutes, never 4.53 hours). Aircraft selection does not recalculate aerodynamic performance; the duration comes from the PDF and is editable.

Plans are queued by UTC departure time, with creation time and UUID breaking ties. Each row projects current aircraft total time plus cumulative estimated duration and cycles, and checks active inspections, installed component requirements, open discrepancies, and the profile status entered on the aircraft record (flagged as “Profile status: …” whenever it is not Airworthy, named apart from the computed aircraft status). Calendar checks use the planned flight-end date, including UTC midnight rollover, with existing end-of-month limits unchanged. Flights extending past an inspection’s calendar deadline show “Due before further Flight.” Reached or exceeded inspection time limits, including cumulative planned flight time, show “Due before further Flight.” Projections assume no intervening maintenance. Estimates do not update actual meters or aircraft totals.

**Log flight** opens the existing flight form with route endpoints, date/time, rules, cycles, and landings filled in. Verify the actual pilot and date/time and enter ending meter readings; the starting readings remain read-only. Mixed IFR/VFR plans default to their initial rules in the log's existing IFR/VFR selector. The log entry links to its plan. Duplicate logging is rejected atomically by the backend, and replay of the same operation remains idempotent. Accepted flights leave the proposed queue. Flights held for review remain in projections until accepted; rejecting the log makes the plan available again.

**Remove** cancels a proposed plan after confirmation and removes its estimate. Logged or held plans cannot be edited or removed. Cancelled and logged plans remain visible in history. Duplicate imports of the same PDF for the same aircraft are rejected while the original plan is active or logged. Cancelled PDFs can be imported again. Plan edits use version checks and save audit entries. Plans follow their aircraft's current Flight Program during transfers and are filtered by aircraft access in snapshots. Creating, editing, and removing plans, and starting a plan's flight, require the aircraft's flight permission and an online connection; offline, **Start / resume flight** on a plan with no saved preflight is disabled with "Starting needs a connection: this device's copy of the plan may be out of date." Starting needs nothing from the server (the preflight is checked against the device's copy and saved in the browser), but online the plan is the server's current one, so a plan another pilot flew, changed or removed since the last sync is never started from a stale copy. Resuming a plan whose preflight is saved on this device needs only the flight permission and works offline: it opens End flight from the saved preflight. Logging continues to use the existing offline flight queue, and the server checks the flight on sync as before, including the atomic duplicate-logging check.

Validation includes a positioned-text fixture and anonymized PDF fixture, domain tests for parsing and cumulative hours/cycles/calendar/component limits, backend tests for authorization and duplicate logging, and a browser test covering import, review, edit, reload, log conversion, and removal.

# FAA requirements for an electronic aircraft maintenance-records app (Part 91, with a Part 135 path)

Researched 2026-11-01 from primary sources only: eCFR (versioner API, current through 2026-10-29, cross-checked against govinfo.gov 2025 edition), FAA advisory circular PDFs on faa.gov, FAA Chief Counsel legal interpretations, Federal Register. Secondary sources were used only to locate documents.

## Summary

For a Part 91 operator the binding rules are 14 CFR 43.9/43.11 (what the mechanic or IA must put in an entry) and 91.417/91.419 (what the owner/operator keeps, for how long, and transfers at sale). None requires paper; AC 43-9D para 8.2 says records may be in any format that preserves continuity, holds the required content, accepts new entries, provides for signature, and is intelligible. Electronic signatures and recordkeeping are governed by AC 120-78B (Dec 11, 2024, cancelling 120-78A). A Part 91 operator needs no FAA approval, acceptance, or OpSpec A025, but the AC is the FAA's stated yardstick and the FAA "may question its validity" if a record lacks the AC's key elements. A Part 135 certificate holder must obtain OpSpec A025 from its Flight Standards office before using electronic signatures or records. Two changes since most secondary write-ups: the falsification rule moved from 43.12 to new 14 CFR 3.403 (effective Nov 3, 2025), and AC 43-9C was replaced by AC 43-9D (Sept 22, 2025).

## 14 CFR 43.9 and 43.11

43.9(a) fields are in the table; the signature "constitutes the approval for return to service only for the work performed." 43.9(c) sends Part 91 inspections to 43.11; 43.9(d) adds Form 337 for major repairs/alterations.

43.11(a): after a Part 91 inspection, the approver records type and extent, "the date of the inspection and aircraft total time in service", signature/certificate number/kind, and the prescribed statement; (a)(7) requires inspection-program entries (Cessna 441 under 91.409(e)/(f); R66 if a program is elected) to identify the program, the portion accomplished, and state it was performed per that program.

Interpretations: an annual or 100-hour sign-off is required only in the aircraft record, optionally also in engine/propeller logs (Stanley, Mar 4, 2015, https://www.faa.gov/media/12041; Easter, May 2016, https://www.faa.gov/sites/faa.gov/files/faa_migrate/interps/2016/Easter-Precision_Design_2016_Legal_Interpretation.pdf). The rules "do not specify what that record must be"; an entry may be made on a separate document and the owner must retain it (Morey, Jan 6, 2017, https://www.faa.gov/media/11336). 91.413 transponder checks are 43.9 entries, not 43.11 (Frelander, Mar 23, 2016, https://www.faa.gov/media/11501).

## 14 CFR 91.417, 91.419, 91.409

91.417(a)(1): description, date, and "the signature, and certificate number of the person approving the aircraft for return to service." (a)(2): the six status items, including AD status "including, for each, the method of compliance, the AD number and revision date. If the AD involves recurring action, the time and date when the next action is required." (b)(1): (a)(1) records kept "until the work is repeated or superseded by other work or for 1 year"; (b)(2): (a)(2) records "retained and transferred with the aircraft at the time the aircraft is sold." The 91.411/91.413 exclusion affects retention period only, not whether entries are made (AC 43-9D 8.2 Note). A "blanket" statement such as "all ADs complied through this date" does not satisfy (a)(2)(v) (Easter 2016).

91.419: seller transfers (a)(2) and (a)(1) records "in plain language form or in coded form at the election of the purchaser, if the coded form provides for the preservation and retrieval of information in a manner acceptable to the Administrator"; the buyer may let the seller keep custody of (a)(1) records but remains responsible under 91.417(c).

91.409(f): the operator of a turbine multiengine airplane must "select, identify in the aircraft maintenance records, and use" one of four inspection programs and name the person responsible for scheduling; turbine rotorcraft may use (a)-(d) or elect (e). (https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-14/section-91.409) 91.173 is the IFR flight-plan rule, not a records rule.

## Falsification and alteration: 43.12 is now 14 CFR 3.403

eCFR shows 43.12 as "[Reserved]" effective 2025-11-03. The final rule "Falsification, Reproduction, Alteration, Omission, or Incorrect Statements" (90 FR 42517, Sept 3, 2025, effective Nov 3, 2025; https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/09/03/2025-16902/) consolidated part-specific rules into Part 3 Subpart D. 3.403 prohibits fraudulent or intentionally false statements, "production, reproduction, or alteration, for fraudulent purpose," and knowing omission of a material fact in "any document in any format" (3.401(b): "documents (electronic or physical)") kept or used to show compliance; penalties are certificate action or civil penalty.

The prohibited act is alteration for fraudulent purpose, not correction. Chief Counsel: the regulations "do not specify the means by which an aircraft's records must be corrected" (Easter 2016); "a certificate holder should take action to correct an inaccurate record when the inaccuracy becomes apparent" (Benninger, Feb 16, 2010, https://www.faa.gov/media/13981). AC 43-9D has no logbook-correction procedure; AC 120-78B 2.2.8 (superseding entry, prior signature invalidated) is the only FAA-stated electronic method.

## AC 120-78B

Current revision: AC 120-78B, 12/11/24, para 1.4 "AC 120-78A ... dated June 22, 2016, is canceled." FAA's AC index lists 120-78B active, 120-78A cancelled. A coordination draft "AC 120-78B CHG 1" (faa.gov/aircraft/draft_docs) drew industry comments in Feb 2026; no final Change 1 appears on the AC page. Applicability (1.5) includes "Operators conducting operations in accordance with 14 CFR part 91" and "Individuals performing maintenance ... in accordance with 14 CFR part 43"; scope (1.6) lists Maintenance Logbook, Maintenance Performed, Airworthiness Release, Maintenance Records.

Approval (1.9): FAA approval only for e-MEL and 121/125 crew records; acceptance plus A025 for 91K, 121, 125, 133, 135, 141, 142, 145, 147; "under 14 CFR part 61, 63, 65, 91 (excluding 91K), 129, or 137 does not require formal FAA approval, acceptance, or authorization ... However, the FAA recommends that all certificate holders follow the guidelines ... If a required signature, record, or manual ... does not contain the key elements or does not follow the guidelines set forth in this AC, the FAA may question its validity" (1.9.5). Inspector guidance for A025 is FAA Order 8900.1, Vol. 3, Ch. 18, Sec. 3 (1.9.3).

Beyond the table: intent statement such as "Signed by" (2.2.2); signer notified when affixed (2.2.3); signature applies only to what the signer reviewed (2.2.4); signing access "under the sole custody of the signer at all times" (2.2.6.1); PKI "typically the most secure" but not mandated (2.1.1). Records: "equivalent or better data integrity, accuracy, and accessibility" than paper (3.1); backups may be "a separate electronic system, a backup server, external drives, and printed records" (3.2.7); direct FAA system access is voluntary but "providing the records themselves is mandatory" (3.2.8.6); 3.2.8.9-3.2.8.12 cover migration, legacy-to-electronic continuity, and transfer under 91.419/135.441. Chapter 5: records must survive discontinuance of the electronic system.

## AC 43-9D

AC 43-9D, "Maintenance Records and FAA Form 8130-3 Return to Service," 9/22/25, cancels AC 43-9C (https://www.faa.gov/documentLibrary/media/Advisory_Circular/AC_43-9D.pdf; index https://www.faa.gov/regulations_policies/advisory_circulars/index.cfm/go/document.current/documentNumber/43-9). 8.2: "any format that provides record continuity, includes required contents, lends itself to the addition of new entries, provides for signature entry, and is intelligible." 8.3: separate engine/prop logs not required but ease transfer. 8.4: description must let "a person unfamiliar with the work ... understand what was done"; referenced documents other than common manuals/SBs become part of the record. 8.6: record certificate type even under 91.417. 8.7: the six status items are the owner's to "verify and validate." 15: lost records need reconstruction, possibly a notarized statement. 16: computerized systems "usually do not meet the requirements of 14 CFR 43.9 or 91.417. The owner/operator who uses such a system must ensure that it provides the information required by 14 CFR 91.417, including signatures ... that responsibility may not be delegated. Refer to AC 120-78"; scanning paper records into a searchable digital archive is endorsed.
